Welcome, Adventurer

Learn how to get started with Dungeons & Dragons. In this game, you create a hero, go on adventures, and experience a story together with other players.

Step 1: Create a Character

In Dungeons & Dragons you play as a hero in a fantasy world. You start by creating a character. This determines what your character can do during the adventure.

Race determines where your character comes from. Some examples:

  • Elf – fast and good with magic
  • Dwarf – strong and resilient
  • Human – versatile and adaptable
  • Halfling – small but very agile

Class determines your character’s role:

  • Fighter – strong warrior skilled in combat
  • Wizard – uses powerful spells
  • Rogue – stealthy and clever attacks
  • Cleric – uses divine magic to heal

Step 2: Ability Scores

Every character has six core abilities. These determine how good you are at different actions.

  • Strength – physical power (attacks, lifting)
  • Dexterity – speed and agility
  • Constitution – endurance and health
  • Intelligence – knowledge and logic
  • Wisdom – perception and intuition
  • Charisma – personality and persuasion

These stats affect almost everything in the game: combat, skills, and social interactions.

Step 3: How the Game Works

Dungeons & Dragons is a storytelling game. One player is the Dungeon Master (DM). DM The DM describes the world, the enemies, and what happens.

The other players describe what their characters do.

Example:

  • DM: "You are standing in front of an old locked door in a dungeon."
  • Player: "I try to break the door open."
  • DM: "Roll a Strength check."

Your dice roll then determines whether your action succeeds or fails.

Step 4: Combat

When players fight monsters or enemies, combat begins. Combat happens in rounds so everyone gets a turn.

Each turn a player can usually:

  • Move
  • Take an action (attack, cast a spell)
  • Sometimes take a bonus action

To make an attack you usually roll a d20. If you roll high enough, you hit the enemy.

Step 5: Dice

Dungeons & Dragons uses different types of dice. Each die has a different number of sides.

  • d4 – small damage or effects
  • d6 – often used for weapons
  • d8 – medium weapon damage
  • d10 – larger damage
  • d12 – heavy weapons
  • d20 – the most important die for actions

The d20 is used for most actions like attacks, skill checks, and saving throws. The higher you roll, the more likely your action succeeds.

Step 6: Roleplaying

A large part of Dungeons & Dragons is roleplaying. This means you play as if you really are your character.

You can talk to NPCs, negotiate with enemies, solve mysteries, and discover stories.

Many players find this the most fun part of the game because the story changes depending on the choices made by the players.
